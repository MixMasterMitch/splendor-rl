# Splendor Advanced Competitive Strategy Report
**Subject:** Transitioning from Engine-Building to Action-Efficiency
**Source Material:** Meta-analysis of tournament play, high-level Reddit discussions (r/boardgames), and WSBG (World Series of Board Gaming) tactics.

---

## 1. Executive Summary: The Efficiency Pivot
In low-to-mid-level play, *Splendor* is treated as an "engine builder"—a game where you buy many low-level cards to make future cards free. In professional play, *Splendor* is a **resource management race**. The goal is not to have the most cards, but to reach 15 points in the fewest number of turns (the "Action Count").

**Key Benchmark:** * **Beginner Win:** 30–40 turns.
* **Pro Win:** 21–26 turns.



---

## 2. The "Tall" Strategy vs. The "Wide" Strategy
The single most important distinction in advanced play is the rejection of the "Wide" (engine) approach.

### The Wide Strategy (Amateur)
* **Action:** Buying 10+ Tier 1 cards to create deep discounts.
* **Flaw:** Each turn spent buying a 0-point Tier 1 card is a turn you didn't spend taking chips or buying a Tier 2/3 point-bearing card. By the time your "engine" is ready, the game is usually over.

### The Tall Strategy (Pro)
* **Action:** Buying the absolute minimum (usually 3–6) Tier 1 cards required to "bridge" into high-point cards.
* **Philosophy:** Use chips as your primary currency, not card discounts. If a card doesn't offer points or a direct path to a 3+ point card, it is likely a waste of an action.

## 4. The Three-Color Synergy (Power Triads)
The game's deck is mathematically weighted toward certain color combinations. To play optimally, you must identify which "triad" is most available on the board and ignore the other two colors.

**Common High-Efficiency Triads:**
1.  **Red / Black / White:** Often has high-point overlap in Tiers 2 and 3.
2.  **Blue / Green / White:** Another common cluster.
3.  **Black / Blue / Red:** High synergy for specific Noble-lite strategies.

**Strategy:** By turn 5, you should have identified your primary 2-3 colors. Every chip you take outside of these colors (unless for defensive hoarding) is a wasted sub-action.

---

## 5. Advanced Reserve Tactics
In casual play, reserving is a "last resort" for chips. In pro play, it is a primary offensive and defensive tool.

* **Gold Hoarding:** In 2-3 player games, the Gold supply is tight. Reserving a card just to take the Gold can "starve" an opponent who needs one more chip to buy a 5-point card.
* **The "Kill-Switch" Reserve:** If you see an opponent has 6 Red chips and there is a 4-point card costing 7 Red, reserve it immediately. You deny them 4 points and "waste" their previous 3 turns of chip collection.
* **Tier 3 Camping:** If an S-Tier 5-point card appears on the Tier 3 row, reserve it immediately if you have the space. Do not wait to collect the chips; a pro opponent will snatch it or reserve it themselves within one rotation.

---

## 6. The "Noble Trap" Analysis
Nobles are 3-point bonuses that require ~8-9 cards. 
* **The Pro View:** Nobles are a byproduct, not a goal. 
* If you focus on a Noble, you are buying many 0-point cards. A player who ignores Nobles and goes "Tall" can often buy three 5-point Tier 3 cards in the same amount of time.
* **When to go for Nobles:** Only if the cards required for the Noble are the *same* cards that give you high points (e.g., the Tier 2 cards that cost 6 and give 3 points).

---

## 7. Chip Management & Starvation
Advanced players track the "Board State Density."
* **The Rule of 3:** If a stack of chips has 4 or more, players can take 2 of that color. Pros will often take chips specifically to drop a stack to 3, preventing the next player from taking a "Double."
* **Stalling:** If you are ahead, you may choose to take 3 chips you don't need simply to stop the board from cycling, forcing your opponent to take inefficient actions or reserve cards they don't want.



---

## 8. Conclusion: The Winning Checklist
To play like a pro, ask these questions every turn:
1. **Action Count:** Does this buy/take get me closer to 15 points than any other move?
2. **Denial:** Is there a card my opponent *must* have to win in the next 3 turns? (If yes, reserve it).
3. **The 25-Turn Clock:** Am I on track to finish by turn 25? If not, stop buying Tier 1 cards immediately.
